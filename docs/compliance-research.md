# Who carries Comatocard's compliance duties

State of the rules as of 11 November 2026. Implemented since: the roles and custody, Indonesia, card and personal data statements in `docs/compliance.md`, curator sanctions reports in the registry with the list named in the risk verdict, fiat valuation in the CSV export, and SDK warnings for personal data and for agent cards without checks (issues #70 to #73). This is factual research, not legal advice. Comatocard holds no licence and has not been assessed by any regulator. Claims taken from secondary sources, or from primary texts that could not be re-read for this report, are flagged in the text.

## Ringkasan eksekutif

Comatocard, sebagai perangkat lunak *self-custodial* tanpa kunci admin dan dengan kode *vault* yang dikunci melalui *hash*, berada di luar hampir semua rezim perizinan yang diteliti, tetapi kewajiban hukum tidak hilang: kewajiban itu berpindah kepada pihak yang menjalankan layanan untuk orang lain (operator *hosted*) dan kepada *merchant*. Penerbit perangkat lunak sumber terbuka tidak cocok dengan peran berizin mana pun di Indonesia (POJK 27/2024 jo. POJK 23/2025), Amerika Serikat (pengecualian GENIUS Act Pasal 2(7)(B), 3(h)(1)(C) dan 10(e); panduan FinCEN FIN-2019-G001), Uni Eropa (MiCA Recital 83, AMLR Recital 160), Singapura, Hong Kong, maupun Britania Raya, selama tim tidak memegang kunci, tidak memungut biaya atas transfer, dan tidak mengendalikan aliran dana. DAO yang membelanjakan kas miliknya sendiri adalah pengguna, bukan penyedia jasa aset virtual, kecuali ia menerbitkan kartu atas dana pihak lain sebagai usaha. Temuan paling tegas justru bersifat lokal: di Indonesia, UU 7/2011 Pasal 21 mewajibkan rupiah untuk setiap transaksi pembayaran di dalam negeri dengan ancaman pidana kurungan hingga satu tahun dan denda hingga Rp200 juta, dan Bank Indonesia melarang penyedia jasa pembayaran memproses mata uang virtual, sehingga Comatocard tidak boleh dipasarkan untuk pembayaran *stablecoin* kepada *merchant* Indonesia atas transaksi domestik. Celah produk yang paling penting ada lima: belum ada sumber daftar sanksi (registry secara eksplisit "bukan daftar sanksi", sementara oracle sanksi Chainalysis sudah dihentikan dan tidak pernah mendukung NEAR); kunci pemilik dan kurator registry yang dipegang tim dapat dibaca regulator sebagai indikator kendali menurut laporan DeFi FATF Juli 2026; label, memo dan referensi kartu adalah teks bebas yang tersimpan permanen di rantai, bertentangan dengan arah UU PDP dan Pedoman EDPB 02/2025; ekspor CSV belum memuat nilai rupiah atau mata uang fiat lain untuk pembukuan dan pajak (PMK 50/2025); dan belum ada bukti kepemilikan *vault* serta paket data pengirim untuk *Travel Rule* yang akan diminta oleh penyedia jasa berizin di sisi *merchant*. Prioritas tindakan adalah dokumentasi peran dan kustodi, pernyataan tegas untuk pasar Indonesia, penafian istilah "kartu", kategori sanksi dan asal daftar pada registry, nilai fiat pada CSV, serta mode risiko bawaan untuk kartu agen. Daftar pantauan memuat teks final revisi UU P2SK (disahkan DPR 4 Juni 2026), POJK *stablecoin* yang ditargetkan awal 2027, berlakunya PP 33/2026 pada 16 Januari 2027, berlakunya GENIUS Act pada 18 Januari 2027, penutupan jendela *savings* Inggris pada 28 Februari 2027, dan sidang ulang Roman Storm pada 26 April 2027.

## Bottom line

**Comatocard's design keeps the publisher outside every licensing perimeter examined, and pushes the duties onto whoever runs the service for others and onto merchants.** The no-admin-key, hash-pinned, self-custodial vault matches the explicit software carve-outs in US law (GENIUS Act Sec. 2(7)(B), 3(h)(1)(C), 10(e); FinCEN FIN-2019-G001 Sec. 5.2.2), EU law (MiCA Recital 83, AMLR Recital 160) and the FATF "software only" position, and none of Indonesia's five licensed digital-asset roles describes a code publisher. A DAO spending its own treasury through cards is a user, not a service provider. A hosted operator is exposed wherever it holds card or approver keys, takes fees in the transferred token, settles to merchants or serves EU, UK, Singapore-based or Hong Kong retail customers, and since 1 July 2026 the EU has no grace period left. The single hard prohibition is Indonesian: rupiah is mandatory for domestic payment transactions under UU 7/2011 Art. 21, so the product must not be positioned as a way to pay Indonesian merchants in stablecoins. The highest-value fixes are cheap: a role and custody statement, an Indonesia statement, a "card" disclaimer, a sanctions category and list provenance in the registry, fiat valuation in the CSV export, and agent cards that check the registry by default.

## Four roles decide the outcome, not the code

Every regime examined asks the same question in different words: does a person, as a business, exchange, transfer or safeguard value **for or on behalf of another person**? FATF's VASP definition turns on that phrase ([FATF Recommendations](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Fatf-recommendations.html)), FinCEN's on "total independent control" over value ([FIN-2019-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf)), MiCA's on services provided "on behalf of clients" ([MiCA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj)), and the UK's on "the requisite degree of control" ([Skadden on PERG 18](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/uks-fca-finalises-cryptoasset-perimeter-guidance)). So this report separates four roles. The **publisher** is the team that publishes the open-source contracts, SDK, indexer and agents. The **DAO vault owner** is a user or Sputnik DAO that owns a vault and issues cards for its own spending. The **hosted operator** runs the dashboard, the meta-transaction relayer and the indexer for third parties. The **merchant** receives stablecoin payments.

Two facts from the repository shape the analysis. First, the relayer sponsors only owner actions on factory vaults, with an allowlist of owner methods and zero attached deposit, and **card payments never go through it** (`apps/relayer/README.md`, `docs/security.md`), which is a stronger "network access service" fact pattern than most relayers have. Second, cards are NEP-171 tokens that can be transferred to another holder, who may then spend within limits it cannot change (`docs/specs/vault-contract.md`). A transferable spending right over a vault's funds is a product feature, but an owner that issues cards to third parties as a business starts to look like a service provider. That boundary needs to be stated in the docs.

## Indonesia: the rupiah obligation blocks domestic stablecoin merchant payments

Indonesia is the only jurisdiction where the research found a **direct prohibition on the core use case**. UU 7/2011 Art. 21(1) requires rupiah for "every transaction that has a payment purpose", and Art. 33(1) sets up to one year of detention and a fine of up to **Rp200 million** ([Hukumonline, quoting the statute](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/begini-legalitas-bitcoin-di-indonesia-lt6039d578c081e/?page=all)). PBI 18/40/2016 barred licensed payment providers from processing virtual-currency payments (same source), and the current payment-system framework requires a Bank Indonesia licence for anyone acting as a payment service provider ([BI, PBI 23/6/2021](https://www.bi.go.id/id/publikasi/peraturan/pages/pbi_230621.aspx)). OJK restated in October 2025 that crypto cannot be a payment instrument, while leaving room for users to sell or swap crypto into rupiah to pay ([Infobanknews](https://infobanknews.com/revisi-uu-p2sk-buka-jalan-kripto-jadi-instrumen-pembayaran-begini-kata-ojk/)). The exposure sits with the merchant and the payer, not only with a licensed intermediary. Self-custody does not cure it. Flows that do not have a domestic payment purpose, such as payments to merchants abroad, agent spend on foreign APIs over HTTP 402 and DAO treasury transfers, remain available, but whether a specific flow falls under Art. 21 is a fact question, and the statutory exceptions in Art. 21(2) were not verified.

On licensing, OJK has supervised crypto as "digital financial assets" (AKD) since 10 January 2025. POJK 27/2024 names five licensed roles: exchange, clearing, custodian, trader (Pedagang AKD) and "other parties set by OJK" ([OJK FAQ POJK 27/2024](https://www.ojk.go.id/id/regulasi/Documents/Pages/POJK-27-2024-AKD-AK/FAQ%20POJK%2027%20Tahun%202024%20Penyelenggaraan%20Perdagangan%20Aset%20Keuangan%20Digital%20Termasuk%20Aset%20Kripto%20AKD%20AK.pdf)). **None describes a publisher of non-custodial code.** An operator that converts rupiah to stablecoins, holds keys or settles to merchants starts to resemble a trader or custodian, and the catch-all lets OJK widen the perimeter without a new regulation. POJK 23/2025 bars trading any AKD not on the exchange's list ([OJK SP 210](https://ojk.go.id/id/berita-dan-kegiatan/siaran-pers/Pages/POJK-23-Tahun-2025-Perubahan-POJK-27-Tahun-2024-Penyelenggaraan-Perdagangan-Aset-Keuangan-Digital-Termasuk-Aset-Kripto.aspx)). The DPR passed a revised UU P2SK on 4 June 2026 that reportedly raises crypto to statute level ([Kompas](https://money.kompas.com/read/2026/06/07/133459026/usai-revisi-uu-p2sk-disahkan-ojk-dan-bi-siap-emban-tugas-tambahan)), but **the official law number and article text were not found**, and claims about new capital requirements are unverified. OJK reportedly targets a stablecoin regulation by early 2027 that frames stablecoins as "transactional instruments, not payment tools" ([Tempo English](https://en.tempo.co/read/2121273/indonesia-targets-stablecoin-regulations-by-early-2027-ojk-says), known only from a search snippet), and a rupiah stablecoin model has graduated from the OJK sandbox ([Blockchainmedia](https://blockchainmedia.id/stablecoin-masuk-agenda-khusus-ojk-indonesia-sudah-punya-eksperimen-rupiah-digital-idrp/)). Because the vault accepts any NEP-141 token through `set_accepted_token`, a registered rupiah token could be supported without a contract change, though whether such a token satisfies the rupiah obligation is an open question.

AML duties under POJK 8/2023 and SEOJK 16/SEOJK.07/2025 (in force 3 July 2025) bind licensed traders: risk-based CDD, KYT tooling, the Travel Rule on every AKD transfer (full data at or above USD 1,000, names and wallet addresses below), immediate blocking against the DTTOT terrorist and DPPSPM proliferation lists, and suspicious transaction reports to PPATK ([OJK FAQ SEOJK 16/2025](https://ojk.go.id/id/regulasi/Documents/Pages/SEOJK-16-SEOJK07-2025-Penerapan-Program-APU-PPT-dan-Pencegahan-Pendanaan-Proliferasi-Senjata-Pemusnah-Massal-Pedagang-AKD/FAQ%20SEOJK%2016-SEOJK07-2025%20Penerapan%20Program%20APU%20PPT%20dan%20Pencegahan%20Pendanaan%20Proliferasi%20Senjata%20Pemusnah%20Massal%20bagi%20Pedagang%20AKD.pdf)). An unlicensed operator is not formally a reporting party, but an Indonesian exchange receiving a vault payment will treat the vault as an unhosted counterparty and ask for the same data. The rest of the Indonesian stack lands on the operator. UU PDP has applied in full since 17 October 2024, and its implementing **PP 33/2026 takes effect on 16 January 2027** ([SIP Law Firm](https://siplawfirm.id/id/publikasi/pp-33-2026-pelindungan-data-pribadi)), while the PDP agency had not been formed as of October 2026 ([Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/pelindungan-data-pribadi-dalam-penegakan-hukum-dan-konfigurasi-beracara-di-bawah-pp-33-2026-lt6ac4acd9db1bd/)); the PP text was read only through law-firm summaries. A hosted dashboard, relayer or API offered to Indonesian users must register as a private-scope electronic system operator (PSE) before use, or face access blocking ([Hukumonline Klinik](https://www.hukumonline.com/klinik/a/kewajiban-penyelenggara-sistem-elektronik-melakukan-pendaftaran-lt598832e6a5643/)). Tax under PMK 50/2025 (from 1 August 2025) removed VAT on crypto deliveries and set a final PPh 22 of **0.21%** through a domestic licensed trader or 1% through a foreign platform ([DJP](https://pajak.go.id/en/node/117834)); how a merchant should treat stablecoins received as consideration for services is not addressed by the sources found, but rupiah valuation of each receipt is needed either way. AI rules are soft law (SE Menkominfo 9/2023, OJK's fintech AI code), and two Presidential Regulations on AI ethics and the 2026 to 2029 roadmap were still pending signature in late October 2026 ([CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/tech/20261030162941-37-781009/ri-atur-ai-sesuai-risiko-komdigi-beberkan-isi-regulasinya)).

What Comatocard already supports maps well onto the Indonesian record-keeping and blocking duties: an on-chain trail with terminal events, the CSV export, merchant deny lists, freeze and revoke in the next block, and human approval above thresholds, which matches the human-oversight theme in OJK's AI guidance. **The gaps are a market-positioning statement for Indonesia, DTTOT and DPPSPM ingestion as registry `blocked` entries, an IDR valuation column in the CSV export, and an operator checklist** covering PSE registration, PDP notice, retention and off-chain erasure, and the OJK and BI perimeter tests.

## FATF and sanctions: control indicators and a missing sanctions feed

FATF defines a VASP by service, not technology, and the 2021 Guidance says persons who only develop or sell unhosted wallet software are not VASPs; however, DeFi "creators, owners and operators" who keep control or sufficient influence can be ([FATF 2021 Updated Guidance](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/guidance/Updated-Guidance-VA-VASP.pdf); the FATF site refused automated fetches, so paragraph-level text was not re-verified). The July 2026 Seventh Targeted Update, read in full, splits DeFi into arrangements with identifiable controllers, arrangements centralised in practice, and truly decentralised ones, and states "the FATF Standards apply to the first two categories" ([FATF 7th Targeted Update, FIAU copy](https://fiaumalta.org/app/uploads/2026/07/7th-targeted-update-on-implementation-fatf-standards-vas-vasps-2026.pdf.coredownload.pdf)). The companion DeFi report lists **governance-token concentration and administrative privileges** as control indicators ([FATF DeFi report](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Virtualassets/targeted-report-decentralised-finance-2026.html)). Enforcement remains thin: 93% of 142 jurisdictions have not identified any DeFi arrangement that qualifies as a VASP, and only two have actually licensed one (FIAU copy, paras 45 to 47).

That checklist is where Comatocard's one weak spot shows. The vault has no admin key, but **the registry contract has an owner who adds curators, and curators decide whether an account is `blocked`**, which a vault in `block_only` or `strict` risk mode enforces (`contracts/registry/src/lib.rs`, `docs/specs/vault-contract.md`). If the team holds those keys, it influences whether payments flow. The fix is governance, not code: publish who holds the registry owner and curator keys, and move ownership to a DAO or to the operator that runs the screening.

The Travel Rule binds VASPs, with a USD/EUR 1,000 de minimis under INR.15 and no threshold in the EU; for unhosted counterparties, the VASP collects data from its own customer and applies risk-based controls ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/024b3d80/new-fatf-guidance-released-on-virtual-assets-and-virtual-asset-service-providers)). Legislation covers 83% of responding jurisdictions, but almost half have taken no supervisory action (FIAU copy, para 24). The revised R.16 (June 2025) carves out card purchases of goods and services ([FATF R.16 update](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/update-Recommendation-16-payment-transparency-june-2025.html)), but **Comatocard cards are access keys moving virtual assets, so the card carve-out does not apply** and the VA rules govern; the docs should say so to avoid confusing reviewers. Pure self-custodial use triggers no Travel Rule duty for the publisher or a DAO. The useful product response is to make the vault easy to prove and describe when a merchant's VASP asks: an owner-signed NEP-413 message proving control of the vault and an off-chain, IVMS101-shaped originator record keyed by the payment `reference`, with personal data kept off-chain. For agent cards, the originator is always the vault owner, never the agent.

Sanctions are strict liability for US persons and apply whether or not anyone is a VASP. OFAC's 2021 industry guidance expects a risk assessment before launch ([OFAC guidance](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/913571/download?inline)), and the BitGo and BitPay settlements made IP geolocation of embargoed users an expected control ([King & Spalding](https://kslaw.com/news-and-insights/ofac-puts-virtual-currency-industry-on-notice)). *Van Loon* (5th Cir., November 2024) held that immutable smart contracts are not "property" under IEEPA, and Treasury delisted Tornado Cash on 21 March 2025 ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/12/federal-appeals-court-tosses-ofac-sanctions-on-tornado-cash-and-limits-federal-governments-ability-to-police-crypto-transactions); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/04/a-legal-whirlwind-settles-treasury-lifts-sanctions)). That protection covers immutable code only, and only binds in the Fifth Circuit; relayers, dashboards, indexers and curator keys are run by persons. Criminal exposure for operators of services remains real: Roman Storm was convicted of conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money transmitting business in August 2025, with the retrial moved to **26 April 2027** ([The Block](https://theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-08-26-tornado-cash-roman-storm-retrial-april-2027-412761)), and the Samourai founders received five and four years ([DOJ SDNY](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/founders-samourai-wallet-cryptocurrency-mixing-service-sentenced-five-and-four-years)). FATF 2026 asks the private sector for "wallet screening, blacklisting, and, where appropriate, whitelisting mechanisms as well as freezing and blocking capabilities" that "can be swiftly updated" (FIAU copy, p.5). Comatocard has every mechanism except the feed. **The free Chainalysis sanctions oracle was deprecated, last updated 18 March 2026, and never ran on NEAR** ([Chainalysis](https://go.chainalysis.com/chainalysis-oracle-docs.html)), so the registry needs an operator-run sanctions source with provenance, and the current `Category` enum (`scam`, `phishing`, `drainer`, `impersonation`, `other`) has no sanctions value. Whether any NEAR account ids appear on the SDN list was not verified. FATF also warns that obliged entities "may be unable to rely on issuer-level asset freeze/burn mechanisms" (FIAU copy, para 35); conversely, vault balances in USDC can be frozen by the issuer regardless of Comatocard's design, which users should be told.

## United States: the GENIUS carve-outs cover the software, not key-holding operators

The GENIUS Act (Pub. L. 119-27, 18 July 2025) excludes from "digital asset service provider" anyone "developing, operating, or engaging in the business of developing distributed ledger protocols or self-custodial software interfaces" and "an immutable and self-custodial software interface" (Sec. 2(7)(B)); exempts "any transaction by means of a software or hardware wallet that facilitates an individual's own custody" (Sec. 3(h)(1)(C)); and exempts self-custody software from custody rules (Sec. 10(e)) ([Pub. L. 119-27](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm)). The publisher fits all three. One nuance matters for DAOs: Sec. 3(h)(1)(C) speaks of an **individual's** own custody, so a DAO or company vault leans on the Sec. 2(7)(B) exclusion instead. The Act takes effect on **18 January 2027**, the same day as the OCC's final rule ([OCC NR 2026-92](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-92.html)); the FinCEN and OFAC issuer AML rule was proposed in April 2026 and not final as of November ([OFAC recent actions](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260408_33)); and Treasury's August 2026 proposal bars DASPs from offering non-compliant stablecoins to US persons from **18 July 2028**, with no comparability process for foreign issuers yet proposed ([Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/treasury-proposes-genius-act-gatekeeping-rules-with-a-path-for-foreign-issuers-102nri4)). The vault is token-agnostic, but US liquidity for USDT-type tokens may shrink, and Sec. 4(e) makes it unlawful to imply a stablecoin is insured or government-backed.

FinCEN's 2019 guidance says the developer of a DApp "is not a money transmitter for the mere act of creating the application", that software suppliers "are engaged in trade and not money transmission", and that status turns on "total independent control over the value" ([FIN-2019-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf), Sec. 4.2, 4.5.1(b), 5.2.2). A relayer that only sponsors owner-signed delegate actions it cannot alter resembles a "network access service"; **an operator that generates or stores card keys for agents, acts as an approver for clients, or takes fees in the transferred token moves toward control**. The 2020 unhosted-wallet proposal was formally withdrawn in October 2026 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/380120/treasury-kills-crypto-unhosted-wallet-mixer-surveillance-rules); Federal Register notice not read directly). DOJ said in August 2025 it would not bring new §1960(b)(1)(C) charges against developers of truly decentralised, non-custodial software absent criminal intent ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250910-doj-signals-approach-to-digital-assets-what-it-means-for-developers-and-platforms)), but that is enforcement policy, not law, and the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act is not law either: the CLARITY Act failed Senate cloture on 15 September 2026 and must be reintroduced in the 120th Congress ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/senate-fails-to-advance-the-clarity-act)). New York's BitLicense excludes "development and dissemination of software in and of itself" ([23 NYCRR 200.2](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-23/chapter-i/part-200/section-200-2)), and most states are moving to the MTMA, whose virtual-currency "control" concept is the test to watch ([CSBS](https://www.csbs.org/node/231461)); the MTMA control text itself was not retrieved.

Consumer law offers no safe harbour but little direct duty. The CFPB's digital payment app rule was repealed under the Congressional Review Act and its stablecoin EFTA interpretive rule was withdrawn in May 2025 ([CRS IF12935](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12935.html); [Orrick InfoBytes](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-05-16/cfpb-withdraws-eft-interpretive-rule/)), so Regulation E does not reach a self-custodial vault, but UDAAP authority remains. Calling the product a "card" invites consumers to expect chargebacks and unauthorised-transfer protection that do not exist on-chain, which is the main disclosure risk for a consumer-facing operator.

## European Union: no grace period for EU-facing operators since 1 July 2026

MiCA reserves crypto-asset services, including custody and transfer "on behalf of clients", to authorised CASPs, and the transitional period expired EU-wide on **1 July 2026** ([ESMA statement, 17 April 2026](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-04/ESMA75-113276571-1679_Statement_on_the_end_of_transitional_periods_under_MiCA.pdf)). Recital 83 excludes providers of non-custodial wallet hardware and software from custody, and AMLR Recital 160 exempts self-hosted wallet providers "insofar as they do not possess access to or control over those crypto-asset wallets" ([MiCA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj); [AMLR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1624/oj); recital wording was not re-fetched and should be checked before quoting). The publisher and a DAO spending its own funds sit on the safe side of that line. The "fully decentralised" exemption lives only in Recital 22, is assessed case by case ([ESMA Q&A](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-data/questions-answers/2552)), and is unlikely to help an operator, because a hosted dashboard, relayer and indexer are identifiable intermediaries. ESMA's 30 September 2026 response to the MiCA review proposed a DeFi definition in operative text with a narrow exemption ([HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/madrid/2026-posts/note-esma-response-ce-mica-october-2026)), and the Commission has proposed moving CASP supervision to ESMA, still under negotiation ([Council WK 4499/2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-4499-2026-INIT/en/pdf)).

Stablecoin choice matters more in the EU than anywhere else. EMTs may only be offered by credit institutions or e-money institutions and are deemed e-money (MiCA Art. 48); CASPs had to stop offering non-compliant EMTs by 31 March 2025, though custody and transfer of them is not prohibited ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-and-european-commission-publish-guidance-non-mica-compliant-arts-and-emts)). Because e-money is "funds" under PSD2, the EBA told national authorities to require **PSD2 authorisation for CASPs providing EMT payment services from 2 March 2026** ([EBA no-action letter](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-publishes-no-action-letter-interplay-between-payment-services-directive-psd23-and-markets-crypto)). An operator that moves EMTs for vault owners or settles to merchants therefore faces both MiCA and PSD2; a relayer that never possesses funds can argue PSD2's Art. 3(j) technical-service-provider exclusion. PSD3 and the PSR were agreed with final texts in April 2026, but Official Journal publication was not confirmed ([MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260430-psd3-and-the-payment-services-regulation-key-developments)). The Transfer of Funds Regulation has applied since 30 December 2024 with no de minimis between CASPs, and above EUR 1,000 a CASP must assess whether a self-hosted address is owned or controlled by its customer ([TFR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1113/oj)); EBA guidelines accept one verification method, including the "Satoshi test" ([Notabene](https://notabene.id/post/ebas-new-travel-rule-guidelines-for-crypto-transfers-what-you-need-to-know---july-2024)). An owner method call or an NEP-413 signature is a stronger proof of control than a Satoshi test, but no EBA text addresses DAO-controlled smart-contract wallets or agent keys. The AMLR applies from 10 July 2027; DORA has applied to CASPs since 17 January 2025 and reaches only an operator that becomes one ([DORA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj)).

Two EU rules reach the product even without a licence. The **AI Act's Art. 50(1) transparency duty has applied since 2 August 2026** and survives the open-source exclusion in Art. 2(12), so the policy assistant and agent messages must tell people they are interacting with AI ([AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)). Payments and policy drafting are not Annex III high-risk uses, but creditworthiness scoring of natural persons is, so the docs should forbid using the assistant to set limits from an individual's credit score. The AI Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) moved Annex III obligations to 2 December 2027 ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes)). Under GDPR, the EDPB's final Guidelines 02/2025 (v2.0, 7 July 2026) say to avoid personal data on-chain, prefer off-chain storage with on-chain references, and run a DPIA, and that technical impossibility does not excuse erasure duties ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_202502_blockchain_v2_en.pdf); paragraph numbers not extracted). **Card labels (64 bytes), memos (256 bytes) and references (64 bytes) are permanent free text**, which is the main data-protection risk the publisher can reduce through SDK defaults and warnings.

## Singapore, Hong Kong and the UK: activity tests catch operators that facilitate transfers

Singapore regulates digital payment token services by activity, and the 2021 PSA amendments, commenced in April 2024, added facilitating DPT transfers and exchange without taking possession ([MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2024/mas-expands-scope-of-regulated-payment-services); commencement date not re-verified). Since 30 June 2025, FSMA Part 9 also catches Singapore-based firms serving only overseas customers, and MAS "will generally not issue" such licences ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/sgp/legal-updates/singapore-s-june-30-hard-deadline-for-crypto-firms-serving-overseas-customers)). **An operator should not be based in, or run sales from, Singapore unless it can obtain a PSA licence**; an Indonesian publisher with no Singapore front office and no transfer service is unlikely to be in scope. No MAS guidance addresses non-custodial relayers. The stablecoin framework is still not law (draft PSA amendments consulted on until 16 October 2026), and once legislated only licensees may use the "MAS-regulated stablecoin" label ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/singapore-publishes-draft-legislation-to-implement-its-stablecoin-framework/)). MAS's AI Risk Management Guidelines (7 October 2026) apply to financial institutions, including their use of agentic AI, with core sections from 7 October 2027 ([MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-sets-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption-by-financial-institutions)). They do not bind Comatocard, but FI customers will need inventory, human oversight and monitoring evidence, which the approval queue and event trail provide.

Hong Kong's Stablecoins Ordinance has applied since 1 August 2025; HKMA granted only two issuer licences (Anchorpoint and HSBC, 10 April 2026) out of 36 applications ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/insights/hong-kong-awards-first-stablecoin-licenses-to-hsbc-and-standard-chartered)). Only listed regulated entities may offer fiat-referenced stablecoins, and only licensed ones to retail, so USDC and USDT are effectively professional-investor-only ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/hong-kong-s-new-stablecoin-licensing-and-regulatory-framework)). **A hosted operator marketing "pay with USDC" to Hong Kong retail risks an unauthorised offer** and should geo-gate Hong Kong retail. No evidence was found of HKMA-licensed stablecoins on NEAR.

The UK regime is legislated (FSMA 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026, passed 4 February 2026) and commences in full on **25 October 2027**; the authorisation savings window runs from 30 September 2026 to **28 February 2027**, and MLR registrations do not convert automatically ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/cryptoasset-regime)). The final perimeter guidance (PS26/18, PERG 18) has "no express exclusion from the safeguarding activity for technology providers"; the test is "the requisite degree of control", and information-only services are unlikely to be arranging, while DeFi is left for future work ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/uks-fca-finalises-cryptoasset-perimeter-guidance)). The publisher's lack of control is well placed; an operator's dashboard and relayer that bring about payments sit in the "arranging" grey zone, with further FCA consultation on arranging expected in Q4 2026 and the DeFi guidance consultation GC26/7 closing 18 November 2026 ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-consults-guidance-uk-future-crypto-regime)). Until 2027, the financial promotions regime applies to qualifying cryptoasset promotions aimed at UK consumers ([FCA 2023/27](https://www.Handbook.fca.org.uk/instrument/2023/FCA_2023_27.pdf)), so README and demo copy should stay factual and offer no incentives. A UK-facing operator must decide before 28 February 2027 whether to apply or geo-gate.

## Card industry and agentic standards: no PCI scope, but agent identity is the gap

PCI DSS applies to entities that store, process or transmit cardholder data anchored on a PAN ([PCI SSC FAQ](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faq/articles/Frequently_Asked_Question/Does-the-PCI-DSS-apply-to-acquirers)). Comatocard cards have no PAN, no network and no acquirer, so **PCI DSS and EMV specifications are out of scope** unless an operator bridges to a Visa or Mastercard program, issues virtual PANs or stores network tokens. Visa and Mastercard now settle in regulated stablecoins ([Visa](https://corporate.visa.com/en/sites/visa-perspectives/press-releases/visa-launches-stablecoin-settlement-in-the-united-states.html); [Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/news-and-trends/press/2026/june/mastercard-expands-settlement-capabilities-to-include-stablecoin.html)), but those programs put a licensed issuer, KYC, chargebacks and PCI-scoped processors between user and merchant, which Comatocard deliberately does not. No primary network text on the generic word "card" was found, so the trademark risk is an inference: avoid network marks, card art that resembles network cards, 16-digit identifiers and "works anywhere cards are accepted".

Agentic payment standards are protocol rules, not law. Google's AP2 uses signed "open" and "closed" Checkout and Payment Mandates, "tamper-evident, cryptographically signed digital objects", to build "a non-repudiable, cryptographic audit trail for every transaction" ([AP2](https://ap2-protocol.org/)). Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol and Mastercard Agent Pay require registered, verified agents with tokenised credentials (secondary sources only; network developer docs were not fetched). **A Comatocard card already behaves like an on-chain open mandate**: budget, merchant constraints, validity window and an approval step that acts as the closed mandate above a threshold, with events as the audit trail and `reference` linking HTTP 402 challenges. The missing layer is agent identity: a card is bound to an access key, not to an attested agent. Comatocard is also a push payment, while AP2 and the network schemes are card-pull first, so it fits best as an x402 or AP2 digital-currency payment method. No regulator in any jurisdiction examined has issued binding rules for AI agents holding payment credentials; FATF 2026 treats AI as a risk amplifier (FIAU copy, para 34), and the FSB and MAS material is non-binding.

## Uncertainties

Several findings rest on secondary sources or on primary texts that could not be re-read for this report, and they should be checked before anyone relies on them. The FATF website refused automated access, so the 2021 Guidance paragraphs on software developers and unhosted wallets, INR.15 data fields and the March 2026 stablecoin report were taken from law-firm summaries; only the Seventh Targeted Update was read in full. The revised UU P2SK, PP 33/2026, PMK 50/2025 and the exact PBI article carrying the virtual-currency ban were read only through press and law-firm summaries, and the OJK stablecoin timeline comes from a search snippet. MiCA Recitals 22 and 83, AMLR Recital 160 and EDPB 02/2025 wording were paraphrased, not re-fetched. US items not read in the Federal Register include the unhosted-wallet withdrawal, the Treasury state-certification rule and the DOJ memos. No regulator anywhere has addressed gas relayers, programmable spending keys held by AI agents, or DAO-owned smart-contract wallets under the Travel Rule, so every statement about those is analysis by analogy.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of Comatocard's compliance story. The question is not whether a smart-contract vault is regulated, since in every jurisdiction examined it is not, but who ends up holding a key, a fee or a customer relationship. Each of the product's strongest design choices (no admin key, owner-only policy, card payments that bypass the relayer) already answers that question in the publisher's favour. The residual risks are concentrated in three places the team controls directly: the registry owner and curator keys, the operator's handling of card and approver keys, and the marketing. Writing those boundaries down costs less than any code change and is the evidence every carve-out depends on.

The second shift is that compliance features are becoming a selling point rather than a cost. Regulators from FATF to MAS now ask obliged entities for exactly what Comatocard enforces on-chain: allowlists and blocklists that update quickly, freeze and block capability, human oversight of automated payments, and an immutable audit trail. What the product lacks is the data those entities need from their counterparties: a sanctions source, a fiat valuation, a proof of control, and an accountable identity behind every agent. Adding those as optional, off-chain layers keeps the publisher out of scope while making Comatocard the easiest unhosted counterparty a regulated merchant, exchange or financial institution can accept.
